00The brief

Score! Hero is the reference for legibility and pace — not for its flat-shaded look. goal.io is lit, has a real shadow map and a full post chain, and should read a generation newer.

The failure mode this document exists to prevent

Arcade drift — saturated traffic-light green, rubber-toy players, a stadium made of coloured rectangles. It has already happened once: mint-green turf was the single strongest arcade tell in the frame, and the fix is recorded in the comment above COL.grass1.

Three constraints govern every decision below, and they are worth stating before any of the numbers. The player is 90 pixels tall at gameplay distance, so nothing narrower than three pixels may carry meaning. The frame is portrait, which makes the camera wide vertically and long-lens horizontally. And the simulation never reads the renderer — art can be changed freely without touching balance.

01Colour

The interface owns exactly three hues. No fourth is ever introduced.

RoleHueWhere it may appear
SurfaceDeep navyEvery panel, card, bar, overlay, letterbox
ActionElectric blueAnything the thumb can touch — and only that
RewardGoldStars, XP, level-ups, the minute clock. Nothing else

Green belongs to the pitch. It appears in the UI only as --good and only as a state — a tick, a positive delta — never as a surface, never as a button. If a new accent seems necessary, the answer is a value change inside an existing hue, not a new hue.

The saturation hierarchy — the rule that actually matters

LayerSaturationWhy
UI action~100%Must win against a lit 3D scene behind glass
Kits62–68%The players are the subject
Turf~41%The ground is a backdrop, not a character
Crowd20–45%Mass, never individual reads

Saturation descends with distance from the ball. If a new element does not fit this ladder it is the wrong colour — including a "nicer", greener pitch.

02Character proportions

Seventeen bones. No fingers, no toes, no twist bones, no facial rig. Anything that needs an eighteenth bone is out of scope.

7.2 heads is the whole call

Life is 7.5–8. Score! Hero sits near 7. Chibi sits at 4–5. At 7.2 the head is about 4% larger than life — enough to carry a face at phone scale without reading as a caricature, which is why these players look like athletes rather than mascots. Do not "fix" it toward realism.

Hands are volume markers, not hands. At gameplay scale a correctly-sized hand is three pixels of noise; a slightly small sphere reads as a closed fist, which is what a running footballer has anyway.

The 90-pixel constraint

At gameplay distance a player is ≈90 px tall. The head is ≈12 px and any facial feature 2–4 px. Four consequences, all mandatory:

  1. No feature narrower than 3 px may carry meaning. Eyes, brows and mouth are value blocks, not lines. They exist for the celebration and replay cameras; at gameplay they only need to darken the right region of the head.
  2. The read is silhouette plus two values — shirt against turf, shorts against shirt. Everything else is decoration.
  3. The waist break is load-bearing. White shorts cut the figure at 0.88 m, and that horizontal is what stops the player reading as a coloured stick.
  4. Hair is a silhouette element, not a texture. Four styles, distinguished by outline shape at 12 px, never by strand detail.

The near shoulder cap stands slightly proud of the deltoid; hands are barely visible with the arms down; there is a faint seam at the knee past ~0.6 rad because the thigh inside the shorts is deliberately not built. All three are visible only in close cameras and store screenshots — worth fixing before any marketing capture, and not before.

03Stadium

A modern ~25,000-seat English club ground, not a superstadium. One continuous bowl with generously rounded corners — never four separate stands.

The rake is built as real steps

A smooth ramp with treads painted on reads correctly from the front and falls apart in silhouette at the top edge — which is exactly where the crowd meets the sky, and exactly where the eye actually goes.

Crowd: 18,000 individual spectators, pre-sorted by colour for draw cost and stride-thinned with distance. Mass, never individuals — no spectator may be identifiable, no faces, low saturation. The crowd is the dominant frame cost, so any new crowd feature is measured before it is kept.

◆ Target — the #1 open visual item

The GL bowl is one tier. The canvas renderer has two tiers plus a facia with banners and is still architecturally richer — the last place the old renderer wins. Target: a second tier above a facia band at ~7 m, the facia carrying club banners and a scoreboard.

04Lighting

One key, one broad ambient probe tinted toward the sky, and a floodlight contribution that rises as the sun falls.

Post chain, in order: SSAO → bloom → depth of field → reprojected motion blur → highlight shoulder → grading → vignette → grain. A separate ambient-occlusion contact pool sits under each figure and is graded independently of the cast shadow, because a contact shadow and a cast shadow behave nothing alike.

Sun elevation is a staging parameter, not weather

9° puts the roof lip's shadow across the pitch. Below ~6° the shadow swallows everything; above ~12° the edge sits behind the camera and the effect is gone entirely. 40° is short shadows and a flat, un-dramatic "just a football match" — correct for an early career level, and correct nowhere else.

  • Never neutral. Every preset commits to a temperature. warm never sits between 0.35 and 0.50 — that band is where a frame stops looking graded and starts looking un-graded.
  • Wet is a sheen, not a colour. It raises specular response and darkens the turf slightly. It must never tint.
  • Haze does the depth work. No fog colour is authored separately — haze pulls distant geometry toward the sky's horizon colour, which is why each sky ramp has three stops.
  • Floodlights and sun are exclusive in feel. Above flood 0.55 the pylons are the key and the sun is fill, and the grade follows: cooler shadows, harder falloff, visible pylon flare.
  • Lighting is presentation only. It never touches the ball — a level's difficulty must not change because it got dark.
Three post-chain traps

RGBA16F needs EXT_color_buffer_float or it fails silently. Do not run a full filmic tonemap over a display-referred scene — compress the top ~20% with a highlight shoulder and leave the rest alone. Reprojected motion blur must skip camera cuts.

05Camera

Twelve metres up, thirty metres back, tilted down 20°. Wide vertically, long-lens horizontally — which is the whole reason the pitch reads as broadcast rather than fish-eye.

  1. Elevation band 15°–25°. Below 15° the box markings collapse into each other and the player cannot judge distance. Above 25° it becomes a top-down puzzle game and loses the "you are the player" read. 20° is the middle, and where it stays.
  2. The ball is anchored low-centre so the thumb never covers it — roughly 35% up from the bottom edge, because the aim line is drawn from the ball. This is the single best-judged decision in the control scheme. Do not re-centre the ball.
  3. Push, never cut, during play. Cuts belong to replay and the miss post-mortem only — and any cut must flag the motion-blur pass to skip that frame.
  4. Every camera move is solved, not keyframed. The miss camera is composed from the actual ball end point and the goal; the replay angle is derived from the near post. Nothing is hand-placed, because levels differ.

The camera beats

BeatBehaviour
StrikeFast lens push
PassSlower push plus lateral drift
GoalGoal sequence, then replay
Replay0.45× speed, low angle behind the near post, broadcast banner, holds on the final frame
MissFrames the goal and the ball's end point together — flight line, the keeper's committed dive arc, and the miss distance in centimetres
◆ Target

Half-time and full-time stings. Kickoff card, goal sequence, replay and miss post-mortem all exist; the rest of the broadcast furniture does not.

06Animation

Thirteen clips, hand-authored as sparse pose dictionaries. Pairwise crossfade only — no blend trees, deliberately.

idle3.40 s↻
brace2.20 s↻
celebrate21.35 s↻
keeperSet1.25 s↻
celebrate1.10 s↻
run1.00 s↻
celebrate30.92 s↻
diveL / R0.62 s—
receive0.46 s—
strike0.42 s—
chip0.40 s—
pass0.34 s—

Gold = one-shot action clip · blue = looping · ↻ carries a per-player phase offset where marked

  1. Pairwise crossfade only. No blend trees. A deliberate ceiling, and enough at this camera distance. Any clip that only works blended three ways is the wrong clip.
  2. Amplitude scaling, not clip switching. Animator.amp / scalePose scale strike, chip and pass by the power of the kick, so a tap and a 30 m/s drive do not play identically. This is the cheapest realism in the whole system — new action clips use it rather than shipping soft/medium/hard variants.
  3. Contact frames are the only frames that matter. strike is 0.42 s — about 25 frames — and gets five keys: plant down, hips loaded, contact at ≈0.26, follow through, settle. Adding keys between them buys nothing at 90 px.
  4. Anticipation is compressed to almost nothing. The animation is feedback for a swipe that has already happened, so the strike leads with the plant already down. A real 12-frame wind-up would read as input lag.
  5. Nothing idles in unison. Eleven players breathing together is the strongest possible tell that they are instanced.
  6. Sim reports, presentation interprets. The simulation pushes named cues — bounce, post, save, block, deflect, net — and has no idea what any of them look or sound like. Animation reacts to cues; it never reaches back.
◆ Target — animation debt

No walk or jog, so standing-to-running is a crossfade across a gap — a 1.2 s jog would close it. No tackle, block reaction or shoulder-barge. No half-time or full-time body language.

  1. Condensed, or it is not this game. font-stretch: 92% drives the variable system faces where a named condensed family is not installed — that is what stops the identity collapsing to plain San Francisco on iOS. ◆ Target: bundle a real webfont. Small work, disproportionate effect.
  2. Tabular numerals everywhere, always. A score or clock that reflows as digits change is the cheapest possible way to look unfinished.
  3. Tracking rises as size falls. 17 px score → normal; 13 px team label → .08em; 10.5 px minute → .14em. Small condensed type without tracking is unreadable at arm's length.
  4. One card treatment. 4.5% white panel, a 1 px hairline at 9% white, backdrop-filter: blur(16px), deep soft shadow. Every panel in the game is this. Depth comes from blur and shadow, never from a second background colour.
  5. 56 px is the button. Below 48 px it fails a thumb. Inner top highlight, slow travelling specular, and it is the only primary button.
  6. The HUD is glass over a lit scene. It must never become opaque — the moment it does, the 3D behind it stops being the subject.
  7. Shield crests, never circles. The crest shape is club identity, and it is the same shape at every size.

The screens: match HUD · level select with difficulty pips derived from measured win rates · pre-match brief · result card · career (levels, skills, achievements) · settings. Regenerate ui-preview.html from the real stylesheet to review any of them — DOM screenshots are impossible in the dev environment.

08Image prompts

Read this before generating anything

These are concept and reference prompts, not a texture pipeline. Nothing generated here drops into the game as-is: kits are painted at runtime from a greyscale atlas plus a tint mask, and the environment is procedural. Use them to lock a look before authoring in Blender, and as reference underlays while modelling.

Append to every prompt
--style raw --ar 3:2 · palette: navy #0b1728, electric blue #0090ff, gold #ffc233, turf #43a259 / #2f7f45, home red #d8324a, away blue #5566d8
Negative prompt, every time
chibi, big head, cartoon mascot, cel shading, flat vector, toon outline, traffic-light green grass, mint green pitch, pure black, pure white, lens flare spam, watermark, text artifacts, fisheye, wide-angle distortion

09Scope & change control

What this guide deliberately does not cover, and what cannot be changed without a redesign.

Out of reach — blocked on assets that do not exist

  • Photographic textures. Everything is procedural or runtime-painted.
  • Recorded commentary and PA. A synth cannot fake it — a content problem, not a code one.
  • Reflections beyond the wet-sheen specular approximation.
  • Cloth simulation. Shirts and nets are rigid or vertex-animated.

Do not propose a feature that depends on one of these without saying where the asset comes from.

Identity — changing any of these is a redesign, not a tweak

The locked ratios (§02), the camera (§05), and the three-hue rule (§01). Changing one invalidates every screenshot and every other section of this document. Everything else is open to iteration.

After changing world colour or lighting, capture with the js/shot.js harness and look at the frame. Numbers passing is not the same as it looking right — several real bugs in this project passed every numeric check.
